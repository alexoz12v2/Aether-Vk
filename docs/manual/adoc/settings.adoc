[[settings-particle-rendering]]
=== Particle rendering
Location:: Settings › Particle rendering
Shown:: committed comet (67P, December 2021)

.Settings › Particle rendering
Particle rendering sets how finely the dust is drawn. A quality slider presets the detail values; the playback preview quality sets how the dust looks while playing.

image::img/settings-particle-rendering--committed--light--tagged.png[Settings › Particle rendering,width=562,pdfwidth=95mm]

. *Particle rendering* -- Section that sets how finely the dust is drawn, live and saved for the next start.
. *Quality* -- Dust drawing quality from 1 (lowest, fastest) to 5 (ultra). It presets the four detail values below it.
. *Playback preview quality* -- Chooses how the dust is drawn while playing and for 2 s after a pause or seek: Off (full render), Smooth, Balanced or Quality.

==== Properties

Quality::
  Dust drawing quality, from 1 (lowest, fastest) to 5 (ultra). Each step draws the dust finer and costs more GPU time.

  Moving the slider presets the four detail settings below (volume, trajectory, haze and temporal reuse). Editing one of them by hand turns the quality to Custom.

  Saved for the next start.

Playback preview quality::
  While the simulation plays, and for 2 s after a pause or a seek, the dust is drawn as a preview: coarser, redrawn every frame (no temporal reuse), with the flow marks moving out of the nucleus. The flow marks are a visual aid: they travel along the real velocity field, faster than the dust at slow speeds; the particles stay where the physics puts them.

  Smooth is the fastest, Quality the finest; Off draws the full render while playing too.

  When the preview ends the full render starts and refines. "Render full" on the viewport starts it at once. Saved for the next start.

[[settings-billboards]]
=== Billboards
Location:: Settings › Billboards
Shown:: committed comet (67P, December 2021)

.Settings › Billboards
Billboards show an imported image in its own window over the desktop, so you can place a telescope image next to the model. This is where you create one and set its position, size, opacity and rotation.

image::img/settings-billboards--committed--light--tagged.png[Settings › Billboards,width=562,pdfwidth=95mm]

. *Billboards* -- Section to create billboard windows that show imported images over the desktop.
. *Texture* -- Chooses the imported texture to show in a new billboard window.
. *Centre (desktop px), X* -- Horizontal position of the billboard centre, in desktop pixels.
. *Centre (desktop px), Y* -- Vertical position of the billboard centre, in desktop pixels.
. *Size (px), width* -- Width of the billboard in pixels, at least 16.
. *Size (px), height* -- Height of the billboard in pixels, at least 16.
. *Keep aspect* -- When on, changing the width or the height also changes the other so the image keeps its proportions.
. *Opacity* -- Opacity of the billboard, from 0 (transparent) to 1 (opaque).
. *Rotation (°)* -- Rotation of the billboard about its centre, in degrees from -180 to 180.
. *Visible* -- Shows or hides the billboard window without deleting it.
. *Image height at the comet (km)* -- Physical height of the image at the comet's distance, in km. When set, it takes precedence over the plate scale.
. *Plate scale (″/px)* -- Angular size of one image pixel in arcseconds. A FITS image fills it from its header.
. *Detect* -- Finds the nucleus in the image as the brightest spot in its central third and fills the nucleus pixel.
. *Nucleus pixel, X* -- Column of the nucleus in the image in pixels, counted from the left. Empty means the image centre.
. *Nucleus pixel, Y* -- Row of the nucleus in the image in pixels, counted from the top. Empty means the image centre.

==== Properties

Texture::
  Imported texture to show in a new billboard window over the desktop; the button next to it opens the window. Import images in the Imports tab.

Keep aspect::
  When on, changing the width or height also changes the other, keeping the image proportions.

Opacity::
  Opacity of the billboard, from 0 (fully transparent) to 1 (fully opaque).

Rotation (°)::
  Rotation of the billboard about its centre, in degrees (−180 to 180). The rotation handle on the billboard does the same.

Image height at the comet (km)::
  The image's vertical extent at the comet, in kilometres (dot decimals), for an image without a WCS (a JPEG, for example). When set, it wins over the plate scale.

  Empty: not set.

Plate scale (″/px)::
  The image's plate scale, in arcseconds per image pixel (dot decimals). A FITS image fills it from its WCS. The image height at the comet, when set, wins over it.

  Empty: not set.

[[settings-viewport]]
=== Viewport
Location:: Settings › Viewport
Shown:: Orbit mode, committed comet

.Settings › Viewport
In Orbit mode the section shows the projection type and its advanced values, the background and orbit-line options, and buttons to fly above the Sun, the comet or the Earth.

image::img/settings-viewport--orbit--light--tagged.png[Settings › Viewport,width=577,pdfwidth=98mm]

. *Viewport 1* -- Section with the camera and display settings of the viewport; the options shown depend on the mode.
. *Mode: UpZenith* -- Shows the camera mode of the viewport. UpZenith (Orbit view): the camera only pans; rotation and zoom are locked. CometOrbiting (Nucleus view): the camera orbits the comet, and zoom is allowed.
. *Show diagnostic panels (all viewports)* -- Shows the camera coordinates, dust history, observer line and orbit debug panels over the viewport, in all viewports at once.
. *Lock viewport for comparison* -- Refuses mouse orbit, pan and zoom and greys out the projection settings, so the view stays matched to a telescope image.
. *Perspective* -- Perspective projection: distant objects look smaller, like a camera; the field of view sets the zoom. Greyed out while the viewport is locked for comparison.
. *Orthographic* -- Orthographic projection: an object's size does not depend on its distance, like a map or a flat telescope image; the half-width and half-height set what is visible. Greyed out while the viewport is locked for comparison.
. *Advanced: projection* -- The field of view (perspective) or the half-width and half-height (orthographic), the near and far clip planes and the units they are shown in.
. *Background stars* -- The stars are a decorative procedural field; they do not match the real sky, and their field of view is fixed.
. *Orbit lines* -- Draws the comet's reference orbit (red), the Earth's orbit (light blue) and the path travelled in this run (yellow).
. *Nucleus from Earth* -- Switches to the Earth observer tracking the comet, with north up and a field a few nucleus diameters tall.

==== Properties

Diagnostic panels::
  Shows the camera coordinates, dust history, observer line and orbit debug panels over the viewport, in all viewports at once.

  For checking the engine; hidden by default.

Lock viewport for comparison::
  Refuses mouse orbit, pan and zoom and greys out the projection settings, so the view stays matched to a telescope image.

  Turn it off to move the camera again.

Projection type::
  Perspective: distant objects look smaller, like a camera. The field of view sets the zoom.

  Orthographic: no perspective, the size of an object does not depend on its distance (like a map or a flat telescope image). The half-width and half-height set what is visible.

  The parameters of both are under Advanced: projection.

Advanced: projection::
  The field of view (perspective) or the half-width and half-height (orthographic), the near and far clip planes and the units they are shown in.

  A billboard's Match camera to image sets the field from the image instead.

Background stars::
  The stars are a decorative procedural field; they do not match the real sky, and their field of view is fixed.

  Auto draws them in every view except the Model (Earth observer) view, which then has a black backdrop. On or Off applies to every view.

Orbit lines::
  Draws the comet's reference orbit (red), the Earth's orbit (light blue) and the path travelled in this run (yellow). The legend lists them in the Model and Orbit views.

Nucleus from Earth::
  Switches to the Earth observer tracking the comet, celestial north up, with a field a few nucleus diameters tall: the nucleus lit by the Sun as seen from Earth.

  At 0 a white surface facing the Sun is drawn white.

Observer on the Earth's surface::
  On (topocentric): the observer stands at the telescope site on the Earth's surface (latitude and longitude below) and turns with the Earth. This shifts the apparent position of nearby objects by diurnal parallax and adds a horizon.

  Off (geocentric): the camera is at the Earth's centre and only follows its motion around the Sun.

  The view never rotates with the Earth. Saved with the file.

  On: the view is levelled to celestial north (J2000), north up and east left, like a telescope image.

  Off: levelled to the ecliptic pole (on the site's side of the equator).

Longitude::

  Comet tracking: keeps the comet exactly at the centre of the view.

  Sun tracking: keeps the Sun at the centre of the view.

  From the surface, while the tracked target is below the horizon, the view faces the horizon where it will rise.

  Restore preset projection re-applies the tracking projection where the target spans 10% of the view.

=== Viewport: perspective projection
Location:: Settings › Viewport

.Settings › Viewport
Advanced projection for a perspective camera: the field of view with its units, and the near and far clip planes.

image::img/settings-viewport-persp--orbit--light--tagged.png[Settings › Viewport,width=505,pdfwidth=86mm]

. *Viewport 1* -- Section with the camera and display settings of the viewport; the options shown depend on the mode.
. *Advanced: projection* -- The field of view (perspective) or the half-width and half-height (orthographic), the near and far clip planes and the units they are shown in.
. *Half-Width* -- Half of the visible width of the orthographic view, in the chosen units: the view spans plus or minus this value around its centre.
. *Half-Height* -- Half of the visible height of the orthographic view, in the chosen units: the view spans plus or minus this value around its centre.
. *Units* -- Unit used to show the half-width, the half-height and the clip planes: AU, km or m.
. *Keep Proportions Locked* -- Keeps the half-width to half-height ratio equal to the viewport's shape, also when the window is resized, so the image is never stretched.
. *Show objects behind camera* -- Orthographic only: also draws the geometry behind the camera plane (within the far distance), as a telescope image does.
. *Restore Proportions* -- Recomputes the half-width from the half-height once, so the orthographic view matches the viewport's proportions again. Available in the orthographic projection when the proportions differ.

==== Properties

Advanced: projection::
  The field of view (perspective) or the half-width and half-height (orthographic), the near and far clip planes and the units they are shown in.

  A billboard's Match camera to image sets the field from the image instead.

Half-width::
  Half of the visible width of the orthographic view, in the chosen units: the view spans plus or minus this value around its centre.

Half-height::
  Half of the visible height of the orthographic view, in the chosen units: the view spans plus or minus this value around its centre.

Units::
  Unit used to show the half-width, the half-height and the clip planes: AU, km or m.

  Only changes the display; the values are stored in AU.

Keep proportions locked::
  Keeps the half-width to half-height ratio equal to the viewport's shape, also when the window is resized, so the image is never stretched.

  Restore Proportions recomputes the half-width from the half-height once.

Show objects behind camera::
  Orthographic only: also draws the geometry behind the camera plane (within the far distance), as a telescope image does.

  Dust behind the camera is not drawn. A perspective view cannot show it.

  The stars are a decorative procedural field; they do not match the real sky, and their field of view is fixed.

  Auto draws them in every view except the Model (Earth observer) view, which then has a black backdrop. On or Off applies to every view.

Orbit lines::
  Draws the comet's reference orbit (red), the Earth's orbit (light blue) and the path travelled in this run (yellow). The legend lists them in the Model and Orbit views.

Nucleus from Earth::
  Switches to the Earth observer tracking the comet, celestial north up, with a field a few nucleus diameters tall: the nucleus lit by the Sun as seen from Earth.

Nucleus exposure::
  Brightness of the sunlit nucleus, in stops (EV): each whole step doubles or halves it; the slider moves in half stops.

  At 0 a white surface facing the Sun is drawn white.
